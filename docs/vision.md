# Project Vision
## What this project is
This project is a small terminal companion designed to offer gentle, pressure-free company. The companion is not an AI assistant. It does not try to solve the user's problems, give advice, increase productivity, or demand attention. It simply shares a small world with the user. The player may pet the cat, feed it, play with it, sit quietly beside it, observe the weather, listen to music, and occasionally discover small events.
Sometimes the cat speaks.
Sometimes the cat brings something home.
Sometimes nothing happens.
All of these are valid forms of companionship.

## Core emotional goal
The project should make a tired person feel:
* welcomed;
* safe;
* unhurried;
* gently accompanied;
* free to leave and return at any time.
The user should never feel guilty for being absent. The cat does not become sick, unhappy, hungry, or resentful when the player does not visit. Whenever the player returns, the cat simply welcomes them back.

## Core design principles
### 1. It accompanies rather than advises
The companion does not tell the user what they should do.
It may say:
> I'll stay here.
> It's quiet tonight.
> Welcome back.

It should not say:
> You need to be more productive.
> You should improve your sleeping habits.

### 2. The world is cozy, not busy
The terminal should not constantly demand input or display events. Silence, stillness, waiting, sleeping, and looking outside are meaningful parts of the experience.

### 3. Nothing exists only to increase engagement
There are no daily streaks, punishments, urgent tasks, manipulative notifications, or fear-of-missing-out systems. Features exist to create comfort, not to keep the user online.

### 4. The companion has its own agency
The cat is not a tool that obeys every player command. It has its own personality, habits, preferences, and small decisions. The player cannot directly control which objects the cat collects.
For example, one cat may pick up a feather because it finds the feather beautiful.
Another cat may leave a fallen leaf outside and say:
> It still belongs to the tree.
These decisions reveal the cat's personality.

### 5. Different personalities are equally valuable
There is no best personality and no optimal development path. 
A curious cat may explore and bring home many small treasures.
A quiet cat may prefer conversation, silence, or watching the world through the window.
A home-loving cat may rarely collect objects but display more indoor scenes and detailed window views.
A social cat may interact more often with birds or other visitors.
None of these personalities is an upgrade over another.

### 6. Personality is partly natural and partly shaped by companionship
Each cat begins with an underlying temperament. Its habits may gradually change through repeated experiences with the player. The player influences the cat, but does not fully control it.
The intended balance is approximately:
* 60% innate temperament;
* 40% habits developed through companionship.
These values describe the design philosophy and do not need to appear visibly in the interface.

### 7. ASCII characters; Unicode world
The main cat and selected recurring characters use a simple ASCII art style. Unicode symbols and emoji may represent environmental objects, weather, seasons, festivals, and small discoveries.

Examples:
* 🪶 feather;
* 🦋 butterfly;
* 🌲 ordinary tree;
* 🎄 Christmas tree;
* 🌙 moon;
* ❄️ snow;
* 🍂 fallen leaf.

ASCII gives the characters identity.
Unicode gives the world color and seasonal variation.

### 8. Suggest more; draw less
The art should leave space for the player's imagination. A cat appearing inside a box can communicate more than a highly detailed picture. The project should use simple scenes that imply small stories.

### 9. Everyone is gentle
There are no enemies, battles, health bars, deaths, punishments, or failure states. Small visitors may be mischievous, but the world remains kind.

## The companion's identity
The project may have a fixed development name, but the cat itself does not need a permanent canonical name. During the first launch, the player may name their own cat.

## Long-term vision
The project may eventually include:

* time and season changes;
* local weather;
* original music;
* random events;
* festivals;
* collections of small treasures;
* detailed window scenes;
* multiple personality tendencies;
* evolving habits;
* multilingual dialogue;
* community-created event packs.

However, every released version should remain complete and comforting on its own. The project does not need every planned feature before it can be meaningful.
